Design notes
  • Four input types: Raw materials arrive from four sources: (1) agro-inputs (soil amendments, containers, packaging) from external suppliers; (2) bioinputs purchased internally from the Biofactory; (3) plants and propagating material from external suppliers; (4) plants and cuttings collected through field scouting in Tierramor's own gardens or surrounding forests. Each arrival is recorded as a separate receipt event.
  • Group ID assignment: Upon receipt, each group of material — a bag of substrate mix, a batch of seeds, a tray of cuttings, an order of pots — receives a unique code. The ID is assigned to the group, not to individual units. This links each input to downstream records: substrate batches, bag/pot fills, and lot creation.
  • Substrate preparation and bag/pot filling: The nursery mixes its own substrates from raw materials. Each formula has a permanent type code; each batch produced gets a unique ID. Finished substrates are held in inventory and drawn from when filling bags and pots. The ready bag/pot inventory is the direct input to lot creation in Phase 3.
  • Germination and establishment phase (conditional): This phase applies only to lots started from seeds or cuttings. After lot creation, the team monitors germination and rooting progress. When the outcome is known, Daniel or Esteban records the confirmed count of established live plants. Failed bags/pots — and the substrate inside them — are automatically returned to raw materials inventory. Lots from wholesale acquisitions or repotting skip this phase and go directly to maintenance.
  • Lot as the unit of tracking: The Nursery does not track individual plants — the lot is the unit. A lot contains plants of the same species and origin. All maintenance records, count events, and graduation history are recorded at the lot level.
  • Live count and mortality: The team periodically performs a full physical count of live plants per lot and logs it in the portal. The system calculates mortality as the difference between the new count and the previous one. Inventory always reflects the current live plant count, not an estimate.
  • Partial graduation: A lot can be graduated in multiple events. For example, the first 50 plants from a lot of 200 may be ready in March, another 80 in May. Each graduation assigns a price category and adds that quantity to the available inventory for sale.
  • Repotting loop: Graduated plants that need to move to larger containers before sale re-enter the production cycle: new bigger bags/pots are prepared in Phase 2, and a new lot is created in Phase 3 with the origin marked as "repotted from Lot X." The original graduated inventory is debited at the time of repotting.
  • Pricing review: Before each quotation, Daniel reviews unit prices. The system pre-populates base prices from the pricing catalog (species + size/age → unit price). Prices can be adjusted per client to apply discounts or premiums. The final adjusted price is captured in the quote record.
  • Lot selection in quotation: Each line item in a quote is linked to a specific lot. When multiple lots of the same species are available, Daniel chooses which lot to source each line item from. This enables precise inventory reservation and full traceability from lot to client.
  • Notion CRM update: Every external client interaction — new quotation created, status change, sale confirmed — must be logged in the Notion CRM by Daniel or Esteban. This keeps the client history current and supports follow-up and pipeline visibility.
  • Automatic inventory debit on dispatch: When an external sale or internal use dispatch is logged, the system automatically subtracts the dispatched quantity from the Available Graduated Inventory. No manual adjustment needed.
  • Internal invoice: When an internal use dispatch is recorded (e.g., plants going to Food Production), the system automatically generates an invoice for the internal client — same mechanism as for external sales. Administration receives this invoice to track the monetary value flowing between departments.
  • Biofactory integration: When a maintenance record includes bioinput application, the system automatically debits Biofactory's inventory — no action needed from Kennedy. Same mechanism as in Food Production.
